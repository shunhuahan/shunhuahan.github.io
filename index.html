About

Working where pipelines break

I'm a Sr. Staff Bioinformatics Scientist at Illumina in San Diego, where I develop computational methods to resolve genetic variation in repetitive and structurally complex regions of the genome. My current focus is copy-aware, haplotype-resolved analysis for TruPath Genome and DRAGEN. Before that I built targeted callers for the alpha-globin locus and for PMS2, two of the harder targets in carrier screening and hereditary cancer research.

I came to this problem through transposable elements. During my Ph.D. with Casey Bergman at the University of Georgia, I studied how TEs reshape the genomes of Drosophila cell lines, and built the software (TELR, ngs_te_mapper2) to find insertions that standard tools miss. Repetitive DNA breaks every assumption a pipeline makes, and I found I liked working exactly where things break.

The through-line is one puzzle: a lot of medically important genetic variation sits where the genome carries two nearly identical copies of the same sequence, and someone has to work out which copy a read came from. In different forms, it has kept me busy for a decade.

Background

When the genome repeats itself

About 7% of a complete human genome is made of segmental duplications: stretches of DNA, at least a thousand letters long, that appear in more than one place with 90% or more of their sequence shared. Some 950 human genes began in them. They are a record of how our genome grew, and they make some of the most important genes in medicine hard to test.

Drawings are schematic. Numbers from Vollger et al., Science 2022, and Marques-Bonet et al., Nature 2009.

Selected work

Reading the duplicated genome

Most of my work is on medically relevant paralogous genes: telling near-identical copies apart and calling the variants each one carries. It grew out of my Ph.D. on transposable elements, another kind of repeated DNA.

TruPath Genome · 2025–now

Haplotype-resolved calling in paralogous genes

Multi-region Joint Detection (MRJD) analyses a gene and its pseudogene together. With TruPath Genome proximity linkage it rebuilds the full sequence of every copy, both haplotypes of the gene and both of the pseudogene, and tells which is which, even where gene conversion has copied pseudogene sequence into the gene.

Standard WGS · 2022–2025

Targeted callers: alpha-globin, PMS2, SMN

Before proximity data, callers built around single loci. For HBA1/HBA2, depth over non-homologous sequence resolves 14 copy-number genotypes, including −−SEA/αα versus −α/−α: both are carriers with two genes, but only the first can pass a chromosome with no alpha genes to a child. For PMS2, joint analysis keeps the reads a standard pipeline discards.

HBA: checked against long reads in 246 samples and Mendelian-consistent in 575 trios. PMS2: 99.7% SNV recall against long-range PCR in 147 cell lines.

Ph.D. · Drosophila cell lines

Reconstructing transposon insertions

Transposons copy themselves into new places in the genome. TELR finds those new insertions in long reads, assembles each one to recover its full sequence and family, and estimates how many of a sample’s genome copies carry it, which matters in the tetraploid Drosophila S2R+ cell line.
